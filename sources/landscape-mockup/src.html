Rock Paper Scissors Fighter

Two fighters, three moves, five seconds to choose. Attack beats Throw, Throw beats Block, Block beats Attack, and each win hits for a different amount. Try the sketch below: it plays the exchange flow from this document against the random computer.

01

Overview

A browser fighting game built on rock paper scissors. Version 1 is single player against a random computer. Online play and an Android app come later, and they shape every technical choice now.

Simple to read

Three moves, one clear winner each exchange, no hidden mechanics.

Satisfying reveal

Both picks lock in, the move icons drop in, and the fighters act it out.

Built for online

The rules never trust the client, so adding a server later changes how moves arrive, not the rules.

Platforms: desktop and mobile web browsers first, then Android by wrapping the same build.

02

Core rules

Winning moveBeatsDamage
ThrowBlock20
AttackThrow15
Block counterAttack10
  • A tie deals no damage. Only the loser of an exchange takes damage, so there is no double knockout.
  • Each fighter starts a round with 100 health, a value to tune in playtests.
  • A round ends at 0 health. A match is best of three rounds, and health resets each round.
  • The computer picks one of the three moves at random, with equal odds.
03

The mind game

Because the three wins hit for different amounts, this is not a coin flip. Throw is the tempting big hit, and that temptation is what players read and punish.

  1. Throw pays the most, so players lean toward it.
  2. Expecting a Throw, the opponent picks Attack.
  3. Expecting that Attack, you switch to Block.
  4. Blocking too often gets punished by Throw. Repeat.

The balanced mix

When both players play perfectly, these are the shares of picks where neither has an edge. Change the damage values to see how the mix moves.

The rule of thumb: each move's share is proportional to the damage of the move it beats. Attack beats the 20 damage Throw, so Attack is picked most. Throw beats the 10 damage Block, so Throw, the biggest hit, is picked least.

Health changes the stakes. With 15 health left, an Attack or Throw win ends the round but a 10 damage Block counter does not. Late-round picks feel different from early ones.

Against the random computer, Attack and Throw each gain about 1.7 net damage per exchange on average, while Block loses about 3.3. A computer that plays the balanced mix, or punishes patterns, would make a stronger opponent later.

04

Exchange flow

Every exchange runs the same way: a 5 second countdown, a simultaneous reveal, then the animation and damage.

  1. PickA 5 second countdown starts (3 seconds is the alternative to playtest). Tap or click Attack, Throw or Block, or press 1, 2, 3. You can change your pick until the timer ends.
  2. Lock inAt 0, anyone without a pick gets a random move. Picks stay hidden until both are locked.
  3. RevealEach fighter's move icon drops in above their head with a bounce, at the same moment for both.
  4. Act it outBoth fighters play their move. The winner lands with a hit flash, a hit sound and a small screen shake. On a tie the moves clash and nobody takes damage.
  5. ResolveThe loser's health bar drains by the damage amount.
  6. NextNext countdown, or a knockout: the round goes to the other fighter, a "Round 2" or "Final round" banner starts the next one, or the match ends with a winner screen and a rematch button.

In online play the timer and the random fallback pick run on the server, so both players see the same reveal at the same time.

05

Look and feel

Two pixel-art fighters stand close together near the center, about one body-width apart, so attacks and throws visibly connect. Health bars and round markers sit above them, the three move buttons below. The screen is landscape 16:9 and scales to fit.

Animations each fighter needs

Art decision. The free packs we checked (Jestaa's free character, LuizMelo's Fantasy Martial Characters, Pixel Moon Studio's Fighter v2) don't look good enough and none include block or throw. Before building starts, we either find better packs (paid ones included) or generate our own fighters with AI image tools and clean them up into sprite sheets. The fighters on this page are placeholders drawn in code.

Move icons can come from Kenney, whose packs are CC0.

06

Audio

Version 1 ships with free hit sounds and one looping fight track. Custom music made with Claude comes later.

Sound effects

  • Countdown ticks for the last 3 seconds and a lock-in sound at the reveal.
  • A different hit per winning move (attack, throw slam, block counter) and a clash on a tie.
  • KO, "Round 1, fight" and a win jingle.
  • Source: 37 hits/punches by Independent.nu, CC0.

Music

  • A menu loop and a fight loop, with mute and separate music and effects volume.
  • To start: a CC0 chiptune loop from OpenGameArt.
  • Later: Claude composes chiptune music as code (for example note sequences played with Tone.js) or writes MIDI files a free tool turns into audio.

Technology

Phaser 3 and TypeScript, bundled with Vite, with the rules in a separate module that a future online server reuses unchanged.

BOTH USE
Shared rules module, plain TypeScript. Decides each exchange from both moves and tracks health, rounds and the match. No Phaser, no timers, no network code.
  • The game never decides a result inside a scene. Scenes only display what the rules module returns.
  • Going online swaps the move source for the server; the fight scene and rules stay as they are.
  • In online play only the server knows both picks before the reveal, so nobody can peek or cheat.

  1. Rules coreShared rules module with tests, played in the console.
