# Answers for the Play Console forms

These are under Policy > App content (the "Set up your app" list on the dashboard). The answers match what the game actually does: no accounts, no ads, no analytics, settings saved only on the phone, and an online server that only passes moves between two players and saves nothing.

## Content rating

Start the questionnaire, enter your email, and pick the category **Game**. Then answer:

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Violence | **Yes**. The fighters punch and kick, and a K.O.'d fighter lies on the floor. It is cartoon/fantasy violence: no blood, no gore, no weapons, no realistic people being hurt. Answer the follow-ups that way (not realistic, no blood). |
| Sexuality, nudity | No |
| Language (swearing, crude humour) | No |
| Controlled substances (drugs, alcohol, tobacco) | No |
| Gambling, simulated gambling | No |
| Users can interact or exchange content | **No**. Online play only sends moves (rock, paper or scissors) between players: no chat, no names, no messages, no user-made content. |
| Shares the user's location | No |
| Digital purchases | No |
| Unrestricted internet access (web browser) | No |

Expect a low age rating (something like PEGI 7 / Everyone 10+ for the cartoon fighting).

## Target audience and content

- **Target age groups:** 13–15, 16–17 and 18 and over. Leave the under-13 groups unticked: picking those puts the app in Google's Families program, which adds a lot of extra rules.
- **Could the app unintentionally appeal to children?** Answer honestly. If asked, the style is a retro arcade fighter, not aimed at young children.

## Data safety

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Does your app collect or share any of the required user data types? | **No** |

That's the whole form when the answer is No. Why it's accurate: settings stay on the phone (Google doesn't count on-device storage as collection), and the online server only relays moves and room codes in memory without saving them or linking them to anyone.
